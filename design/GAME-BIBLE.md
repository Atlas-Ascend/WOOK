# WOOK — FULL GAME BIBLE

## Executive Product Definition
WOOK is a seven-act top-down comedy adventure/RPG authored in GB Studio. It begins with the smallest possible crisis—missing Crocs—and escalates until Papa Wook is negotiating with memory, responsibility, friendship, logistics, a raccoon, a parking lot, the American road, the desert, and eventually the Cosmic Wook Dimension.

The joke is structural: ordinary festival logistics receive the dramaturgical weight normally reserved for saving kingdoms.

The heart is also structural: Papa Wook slowly becomes dependable without becoming less weird.

## Player Fantasy
You are not the chosen one.
You are the person who eventually remembers where the van is.

## Tone Pillars
- Affectionate satire, never contempt.
- NPCs are memorable because they have motives, not because they are punchlines.
- Mundane needs become RPG systems.
- The world rewards generosity, observation, improvisation, and absurd competence.
- Dialogue should frequently sound like profound wisdom until the final noun makes it about a charger, a tarp, a Croc, or a Walmart receipt.

## Character Arc
Act I Papa Wook: asks everyone for a lighter.
Act VII Papa Wook: brought the water, packed out the trash, found the new kid's friends, charged the power bank, and still asks somebody for a lighter.

## Game Loop
EXPLORE -> NOTICE -> TALK -> TRADE -> GROUNDSCORE -> CHOOSE -> CONSEQUENCE -> QUEST UPDATE -> RESPONSIBILITY +1 -> GO SOMEWHERE WEIRDER

## Mechanical Layers
### Battery
Starts at 17. Phone, GPS, flashlight, camera, and some dialogue paths drain it.

### Vibes
Social reputation and trust. Opens camps, favors, rumors, rides, and alternate dialogue.

### Groundscore
Scavenging probability + world-state event system. Tiny junk may become future quest keys.

### Responsibility
The game's stealth progression stat. Higher Responsibility unlocks better resolutions and more people trusting Papa Wook.

### Wook Karma
Long-memory moral/social state. Separate from Vibes: you can be popular and still have terrible karma.

### Cash
Small-scale resource pressure. Mostly disappears into snacks, gas, charging cables, and decisions.

### Inventory
Deliberately clutter-prone. Carrying junk is sometimes the correct strategy.

### Phone
Contacts, messages, GPS, quest log, flashlight, camera. Useful enough to make 17% genuinely terrifying.

### Choice Encounters
No default attack grammar. Encounters offer contextual verbs:
OFFER SNACK / INTIMIDATE / DISCUSS BOUNDARIES / ACCEPT LOSS

## Acts
### Act I — The Campground
Primary quest: WHERE ARE MY SHOES?
Learn exploration, dialogue, Groundscore, Vibes, Battery, Phone, encounters.
Boss-equivalent: Raccoon.

### Act II — The Festival
Primary quest: WHERE IS EVERYONE?
Crowd navigation, performance timing, friend-finding, stage zones, lost-and-found.
Major setpiece: wrong-stage realization after an emotionally sincere 12-minute detour.

### Act III — The Parking Lot
Primary quest: WHERE IS THE CAR?
Vehicle clues, row/zone memory, battery crisis, Space Dave IOU economy.
Boss-equivalent: identical silver Subaru cluster.

### Act IV — The Town
Primary quest: WHY ARE WE AT WALMART?
Town NPC reactions, supply list, public-space comedy, logistics.
Major choice: buy one sensible thing or seven fascinating things.

### Act V — The Road
Primary quest: WHOSE VAN IS THIS?
Road map, gas, passengers, roadside weirdness, friendship conversations.
Major mechanic: van ownership confidence meter.

### Act VI — The Desert
Primary quest: WHY IS TREVOR HERE?
Heat, water, navigation, silence, Sage Trevor, responsibility payoff.
The game becomes less frantic and more reflective without losing comedy.

### Act VII — The Cosmic Wook Dimension
Primary quest: OH.
Metaphysical remix of all prior systems.
Battery becomes Attention.
Vibes becomes Resonance.
Groundscore becomes Memory.
Responsibility stays Responsibility because Papa Wook is horrified to learn that one was real.

## Ending
Papa Wook returns to an ordinary campground at dawn.
A new kid asks:
"Hey, do you know where everyone went?"
Papa Wook looks at the charged power bank in his hand.
He says:
"Yeah, actually."
Then:
"Do you have a lighter?"
