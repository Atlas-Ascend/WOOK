# ENCYCLOPEDIA OF WOOK

Campaign: `WOOK-ENCYCLOPEDIA-COMMAND-TO-PROOF-001`

## 1. Product identity

WOOK is an original cartridge-class social-adventure RPG and comedy Hero's Journey set across a fictional bass-music festival weekend. The opening problem is intentionally microscopic: Papa Wook wakes in General Admission camping with a phone at 17% and both Crocs missing. The game expands from that problem into a temporary city, a full music-festival weekend, night-side-quest chaos, deterministic alternate-reality spaces, a consequential Ordeal, mastery, goodbye, a road home, a cosmic integration dungeon, and the final answer: `OH.`

The production bar is not “good for GB Studio.” The bar is the intentionality associated with exceptional Nintendo-era commercial adventure software: readable silhouettes, satisfying movement, coherent maps, authored text, secrets, memorable characters, mechanical callbacks, robust state, strong audio pacing, recoverable failure, save integrity, and deliberate presentation.

## 2. Canonical cast

### Papa Wook
Player character and Hero. Visual anchors: hat mass, round sunglasses, substantial beard, layered clothing, utility pack, grounded silhouette. Starts scattered, underprepared, funny, reactive, and dependent on others. Ends still funny but materially more capable: knows camp, carries water, keeps promises, remembers routes, helps friends, solves practical problems, and can be depended on.

### Sniffany
Principal continuity character across camp, venue, 4 AM, Ordeal, later festival states, and the emotional return. Sniffany is not a tutorial dispenser; she has independent objectives, relationship state, callbacks, and evolving dialogue.

### Train Station
Threshold/logistics oracle. Mechanically associated with routes, camp-to-venue transitions, timing, meetup logic, lost/found movement, and later road-home callbacks. Train Station embodies movement between worlds.

### Loki
Trickster and route-corruption catalyst. Loki never simply “explains the alternate reality.” Loki changes expectations, routes, item interpretations, dialogue context, and player confidence while the underlying state machine remains deterministic.

### The Wizard
Liminal mentor. Associated with dungeon interpretation, alternate-reality grammar, symbolic callbacks, late-night stillness, and the cosmic return. The Wizard gives useful guidance without becoming a lore dump.

### Bufo D' Clown
Performance-chaos engine. Venue character, social collision generator, comic spectacle, minigame delivery mechanism, and callback machine. Bufo's chaos must still produce gameplay structure.

### Handstand Dan
Physical-comedy ally and challenge NPC. Owns short skill checks, balance/timing minigames, camp challenges, mastery callbacks, and a recognizable pose language.

## 3. Recurring secondary cast

Moonbeam, Dr. Bronner, Crystalpher, Amethyst, and Moldavite Mike are recurring secondary characters with chapter-specific functions rather than disposable name jokes.

The reserve Wookie roster remains available for GA, venue, dungeon, roadside, and cosmic casting. Named characters only earn screen time when they own at least one meaningful mechanic, quest, state transition, relationship, recurring gag with payoff, dungeon role, or Hero's Journey function.

## 6. World architecture

WOOK uses persistent geography. The Questionable Campground, GA Main Lane, Long Walk, Festival Concourse, Art Grove, Parking Edge, and other important spaces recur under different world phases. Day, night, 4 AM, post-Ordeal, final morning, and teardown alter NPC populations, props, audio, collision gates, quests, dialogue, palette/lighting, affordances, and secrets while preserving enough geometry for the player's spatial memory to matter.

The world should feel temporarily permanent. By C12, the player must feel the disappearance of the festival city because they learned it well enough to call it home.

## 7. Core systems

### Battery
Practical resource and narrative pressure. Controls convenience of phone map, messages, schedule, camera/memory and coordination. A dead phone may create friction but may never be a single point of failure for critical progression.

### Vibes
Momentary social/emotional momentum. It may influence flavor dialogue, animations, optional interactions, music stingers and minigame forgiveness. It is not morality and not a mental-health score.

### Responsibility
Hidden Hero statistic. Increases through preparation, water, cleanup, returning objects, honoring commitments, helping people and following through. It changes options, trust, Ordeal routes, and late-game competence.

### Wook Karma
Reciprocity and social memory. It records whether Papa shares, returns, trades fairly, helps, and keeps promises. It is not a good/evil meter; it powers callbacks.

### Groundscore
Improvisational resource economy. Weird objects found earlier can become useful later. Groundscore rewards exploration and creates alternate problem-solving routes.

### Cash / Trade
Traditional money plus barter and reputation. Vendors can care about cash, Groundscore, promises, affinity, prior help, or specific strange items.

### Affinity
Character-specific relationship state. It determines optional routes, callbacks, who appears during the Ordeal, who joins final scenes, and which goodbye conversations exist.

### Reality Stability
Hidden world-state parameter used to alter routes, dialogue, audio and visual expectations during C04–C06 and again during the road/cosmic return. It is not displayed as a sanity meter.

## 8. Interface architecture

HUD modes: EXPLORATION, VENUE, DUNGEON, ENCOUNTER, MINIGAME, QUIET, CUTSCENE, COSMIC.

Exploration HUD defaults to sparse information: Battery, Vibes, critical quest counter when relevant, contextual Cash/Groundscore. Responsibility, Wook Karma, affinity, full inventory and historical quest information live behind menus.

START opens the WOOK menu: QUESTS, INVENTORY, PHONE, PEOPLE, MAP, WEEKEND, BACK as the game matures. SELECT may open Phone directly when the phone is functional.

Quiet scenes deliberately remove nonessential HUD information. Cosmic scenes may transform the HUD visually while preserving deterministic state semantics.

## 9. Phone

The phone is a diegetic menu system with Battery, Messages, Map/GPS, Weekend schedule, Contacts/People, Notes, Camera/Memories and limited utility features. Its loss of power gradually forces the player to rely on spatial memory, social navigation and competence.

## 10. Inventory

Inventory must distinguish ordinary supplies, quest items, strange Groundscore objects, persistent tools, temporary festival items and symbolic late-game items. Croc Left and Croc Right are canonical early quest items. Item duplication and reward duplication after save/load are release blockers.

## 11. Quest taxonomy

MAIN — mandatory Hero's Journey progression.

CAMP — home-base and practical community tasks.

PEOPLE — relationship and character continuity.

WEIRD — surreal, comic and unexpected chains.

DUNGEON — challenge-space progression.

MINIGAME — contextual challenge loops.

MEMORY — optional captures/callbacks that influence late-game presentation.

## 12. Encounters

WOOK's encounter grammar is social and contextual rather than default combat. The raccoon establishes this immediately with OFFER SNACK, INTIMIDATE, DISCUSS BOUNDARIES, ACCEPT LOSS. Later encounters may use negotiation, trading, rhythm, route knowledge, promises, resource sacrifice, humor, timing or environmental manipulation.

## 13. Dungeons

### Porta-Potty Labyrinth
C05. Orientation, looping corridors, switch/door state, item state, Wizard guidance, Loki misdirection and a noncombat navigation/social boss-equivalent. The surface joke may be absurd; the puzzle grammar must be legitimate.

### Totem Forest
C08. Venue-navigation dungeon built from line-of-sight obstruction, landmark memory, audio localization, route triangulation, friend-beacon state and hidden art paths.

### Memory Constellation
C14. Final integration dungeon. Rooms use accumulated state from Battery, Water, Crocs, Friendship/Affinity, Responsibility, Wook Karma, Groundscore and the Ordeal. It is not a trivia quiz; prior play changes room behavior.

## 14. Minigames

Drum Circle rhythm, Handstand Dan balance/timing, stage crowd-response, flow-art timing, camp setup, secret-set rhythm and other minigames must arise from world fiction. Every minigame needs dedicated input grammar, HUD mode, deterministic completion/failure, recovery rules and presentation QA.

## 15. Audio grammar

The venue is heard before it is seen: silence → distant sub → kick → crowd bed → full stage arrangement. Camp at night can contain multiple distant sources. Alternate Reality mutates known motifs. 4 AM removes almost everything. The Ordeal recontextualizes earlier themes. The final set converges motifs. The road begins ordinary and gradually becomes cosmic. Memory Constellation transforms familiar music rather than relying only on new themes.

## 16. Visual grammar

World art is authored as navigable space, not merely background illustration. Requirements: readable traversal, collision implied by art, distinct landmarks, foreground layering, animated focal points, character/world value separation, environmental storytelling, and state-dependent variation.

Characters require silhouette readability at native resolution. Papa Wook is Tier A hero fidelity; principal recurring NPCs are Tier B; supporting named NPCs Tier C; creatures/encounters Tier D. No default template sprite remains visible in a qualified scene.

## 17. Save and persistence

Checkpoint families: map entry, dungeon entry, dungeon completion, major quest completion, day transition, 4 AM, Ordeal start/end, final-day transition, road-home start. Save schema carries version metadata. Reload must restore map/spawn family, quest state, inventory, social flags, world phase and one-time reward guards.

## 18. Failure law

No critical dependency may have only one fragile solution. Critical paths require recovery routes. Failure testing deliberately includes dead phone, missed optional items, wrong route, repeat interactions, boundary collisions, menu cancellation, save during transition, map revisit after mutation and alternate orderings.

## 19. Platform strategy

WOOK GB remains the proven native baseline and authenticity edition. WOOK DX is the next controlled visual experiment after the Golden Slice qualifies; it reuses gameplay/state truth while increasing palette and scene differentiation. A future WOOK 16 renderer is permitted only if literal 16-bit rendering becomes a hard requirement. Canon, IDs, quests, items, dialogue source and state semantics remain renderer-independent.

## 20. Definition of complete

A map that renders is not complete. A script that runs is not complete. An asset that exists is not complete. A compiler PASS is not a game PASS.

Readiness: SRL-0 Idea; SRL-1 Requirement; SRL-2 Architecture; SRL-3 Native Resource; SRL-4 Compiles; SRL-5 Functional; SRL-6 Failure Tested; SRL-7 Presentation Qualified; SRL-8 Regression Qualified; SRL-9 Release Proven.

A chapter cannot be called complete below SRL-8. The whole game is release-proven only after every critical chapter is SRL-8+, whole-game save/load and route/state regression pass, native artifacts are reproducible, hashes and release commit are recorded, publication is verified, and the final receipt states `WOOK_CARTRIDGE_CLASS_RELEASE_PASS`.
